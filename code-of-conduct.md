# Code of Conduct — Tool Library

A lending collective only works when trust is the default. This code defines how we
treat each other across every project space — the platform itself (listings, messages,
profiles), GitHub (issues, pull requests, reviews), the community Matrix room, the
wiki, and any in-person workshops or events associated with the project.

It is enforced alongside the [Terms of Service](src/app/terms/page.tsx) and mirrors the
in-app governance page at `/code-of-conduct`.

---

## 1. The Four Pillars

### 1.1 Mutual Aid Respect

Every member here is a neighbor lending a hand, not a customer or a service worker.
Say thank you, return tools clean, communicate when plans change, and remember that
the person on the other side of a listing is taking a real risk by trusting the
community with their property.

### 1.2 Zero Tolerance Harassment Policy

Harassment, slurs, threats, sexual advances, doxxing, and intimidation are prohibited
in all messages, listings, profiles, issues, pull requests, and chat. One warning may
be issued for a minor first offense at moderator discretion; severe violations result
in immediate account suspension. We are building a workshop culture where everyone
feels safe picking up a wrench.

### 1.3 Workshop Culture

Teach, don't gatekeep. If a borrower is unfamiliar with a tool, share knowledge
patiently. If you see an unsafe practice, speak up kindly. If a contributor's code or
patch needs work, critique the code — never the person. Skill-sharing is half of this
platform's purpose; condescension and gatekeeping starve the commons that all of us
depend on.

### 1.4 Moderation Accountability

Administrators act only against accounts and listings, never against the community's
right to know. Every moderation action (listing removal, account suspension) is
expected to be explainable to the affected member. If you believe a moderation
decision was wrong, raise it in the Matrix room — decisions are reviewable and
reversible.

## 2. Everyday Expectations

- Describe tools and skills honestly, including known defects.
- Honor loan dates you accept, or renegotiate before the deadline.
- Keep messages on-platform and on-topic; no spam or solicitation.
- Respect that aliases may be pseudonymous — do not press for real identities.
- Report safety issues with tools immediately to the owner and moderators.
- In project spaces, assume good faith and stay on topic; keep discussions about the
  work, not the workers.

---

## 3. Privacy Is Part of Conduct

This platform is deliberately zero-PII: accounts are only a `username` and a public
`alias`, and no email addresses, phone numbers, or IP addresses are collected. Conduct
rules protect that design:

- Never ask another member for their real name, email, phone number, location, or any
  other identifying information.
- Never publish, link, or speculate about another member's identity or off-platform
  accounts (doxxing, including "mild" correlation, is a severe violation).
- Do not attempt to infer identity from message metadata, timing, or platform behavior.

---

## 4. Unacceptable Behavior

The following are prohibited everywhere this code applies:

- Harassment, hate speech, slurs, personal attacks, or intimidation.
- Threats of violence, and sexual language or advances of any kind.
- Doxxing or pressuring members to reveal personal information.
- Deliberately unsafe advice about tools, electrical work, fabrication, or any
  hazardous activity.
- Dishonest listings (misrepresented condition, undisclosed defects) or bad-faith
  loan behavior.
- Spam, advertising, crypto solicitation, and recruitment for external services.
- Sharing other members' messages or listings outside the platform without consent.
- Attempting to manipulate trust scores, exploit the loan system, or interfere with
  the platform's operation.

## 5. Reporting & Enforcement

**How to report:** message any administrator through the platform's built-in
messenger, or post in the public Matrix room
(<https://matrix.to/#/#tools-library:matrix.org>). Include what happened and, if
relevant, the involved alias. Because the platform stores no contact information, all
follow-up happens on-platform or in Matrix.

**What happens next:**

1. Reports are reviewed by at least one administrator.
2. Minor first offenses may receive a single warning, at moderator discretion.
3. Severe violations (threats, doxxing, sexual harassment, repeated harassment)
   result in immediate account suspension.
4. Every enforcement action is expected to be explainable to the affected member.
5. Decisions are reviewable and reversible: raise appeals in the Matrix room, where
   the community can weigh in.

**Good-faith reporting:** reports made in good faith are never penalized, even if the
investigation does not result in action. Knowingly false reports are themselves a
violation.

**Moderator accountability:** moderation exists to protect the commons, not to police
thought. Administrators are subject to this same code; abuse of moderation power is a
severe violation and is raised and appealed through the same Matrix review process.
